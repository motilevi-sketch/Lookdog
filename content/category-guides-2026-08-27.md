## Positions taken

These are the passages that make the guides worth linking to, and the ones
to preserve if the articles are ever edited.

**Grooming.** Tools are bought for coats, not breeds. Never bathe a matted
dog — water tightens mats like a knot in wet rope. Never shave a double coat
to cool a dog; the undercoat insulates against heat too and can grow back
patchy. A groomer clipping a badly matted dog short is a rescue, not a
failure. Anaesthetic-free dental scaling polishes the visible surface and
leaves the disease under the gumline.

**Travel.** A 20kg dog in a 30mph impact arrives with roughly the force of a
500kg load. The overwhelming majority of pet car harnesses sold anywhere have
never been crash tested, and "safety certified" has no standard behind it —
stated plainly *about products this site lists*, with the honest conclusion
that a crash-tested crate is the only tested option and we do not sell one.
A car at 22C outside reaches ~47C inside within an hour. Never tether to a
collar.

**Beds.** "Orthopaedic" is unregulated; press the foam with your palm and see
whether you reach the floor. Fleas live in the bedding, not on the dog, which
is why treating the dog alone never ends an infestation. Gel cooling mats work
for about an hour and are then body temperature. Always leave a route off any
heated or cooled surface. Repeated bed destruction is a behaviour question,
not a durability one.

**Trackers and tech.** Bluetooth tags show where the dog *was*; GPS shows where
it *is* — different technologies, not price tiers. No tracker replaces a
microchip with current details, and out-of-date chip records are the most
common reason a chipped dog is not reunited. **We do not list shock collars**,
and the guide says why: banned in Wales and several European countries, and the
research shows no advantage over reward-based training plus more stress
behaviour. Ultrasonic deterrents interrupt but do not teach, and make
fear-based barking worse.

## Editorial rule these follow

Every guide names at least one thing the category cannot do, and at least one
case where the honest answer is "not this product" — a groomer, a vet, a
crash-tested crate we do not sell, a behaviourist. An affiliate guide that
only says yes is not a guide.
