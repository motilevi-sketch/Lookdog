# A Puppy's First Six Months: What You Actually Need, and When

## The argument the article is built on

Puppy content is the most over-monetised corner of the pet internet. The
standard article is a thirty-item shopping list with a paragraph on top,
because thirty items sells better than nine and because "essential" is a free
word. The angle here is the opposite one and it is the only angle a site with
no first-hand testing can honestly take: arrange the same six months by *when*
each thing becomes useful, and name the things nobody needs.

## Where it argues against a sale

Six places, which is the point:

- **Do not buy the proper bed yet.** You cannot tell at ten weeks whether you
  have a dog that curls or one that sprawls, and that decides the bed shape.
- **Most young puppies do not need a slow feeder.** They need one only if they
  inhale a meal in under a minute.
- **Nothing sold to stop a puppy biting works.** Not sprays, not bitter gels,
  not deterrent devices. Redirection and sleep are the whole of it.
- **Pads have a cost.** Every pad teaches a puppy that indoors is sometimes
  acceptable, which is a lesson you later have to undo.
- **Skip the starter bundle.** Four things at the wrong size and eight you do
  not want.
- **Some of it is a vet call.** Diarrhoea past a day, repeated vomiting,
  lethargy, a persistent cough. Nothing on this site treats any of it.

## Facts checked before publishing

- The Control of Dogs Order 1992 requires a dog in a public place to wear a
  collar bearing the owner's name and address.
- Microchipping is compulsory in England, Scotland and Wales from eight weeks.
- Human toothpaste is unsafe for dogs: fluoride, and frequently xylitol.
- Teething runs roughly twelve weeks to six months; the socialisation window
  closes around sixteen weeks.

Vaccination timing is deliberately left to "your vet sets it, not the
internet" — schedules vary by vaccine and country and this site should not be
the source for one.

## Art direction: the `primer` treatment

The seventh treatment, and the first added since the original six. A
first-timer being sold thirty things does not need a page that performs
authority; they need one that is easy to scan and hard to panic at. So: an
oversized opening paragraph, section headings on a tinted band with an accent
rule so the timeline reads at a glance, and a table ruled only horizontally so
it reads as a checklist rather than a specification.

Accent olive `#3F6212` (`#365314` for links and hover), added to DESIGN.md as
`guidePrimer` / `guidePrimerDark`. It is close to the beds guide's sage
`#4F6152`, which is worth naming: sage is a grey-green for a guide about rest,
olive a yellow-green for one about growth. One accent per page means the two
are never seen together.
