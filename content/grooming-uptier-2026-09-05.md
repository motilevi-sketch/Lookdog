Eight products published into Grooming (term 71), chosen by the scout from the

## Why these eight, and not the other 58

The 66 were not 66 products. They were about a dozen products in many
listings: twelve Fenice scissor sets, five ROJECO nail grinders, six bath
hammocks, and a long run of near-identical clippers. Publishing them all would
have padded the category without widening it.

So the pick was one item per distinct product type, weighted towards types the
catalogue did not carry at any price.

## Two disclosures written into the copy

Rule 5 of the house voice says to state it on the page when a listing sits
below the usual bar.

- **5223**, the Type-C clipper, carries 94.9% positive feedback — lower than
  most items on the site. Said so in *Things to Consider*.
- **5244**, the vacuum kit, has 74 orders — a thin record next to the rest of
  the catalogue. Said so in *Cons*.

## The guide got one link, not eight

`dog-grooming-at-home-coat-types-tools` (4497) already ran seven product links
across 1,794 words, which is past the one-per-800-words guidance. Eight more
would have turned a guide into a catalogue.

One link was added, where the existing sentence already made the argument:

> Clippers are faster, a grinder is safer on black nails, and **quieter
> grinders** matter because the noise is what most dogs object to

Two placements were considered and rejected, both on the rule that a product
link never sits in a passage arguing against that product:

- The *When to Use a Professional* list tells readers to hand face and foot
  clipping to a groomer if they are not confident with scissors near a moving
  dog. Linking shears there sells against the site's own advice.
- The nails section tells readers whose dog hates having their feet touched to
  spend a fortnight on desensitisation. Linking a restraint hammock there
  contradicts both that advice and this product's own copy, which says plainly
  that a dog objecting to paw handling has a training problem, not an
  equipment one.

**The real gap this exposed:** the grooming guide covers coats, brushing,
matting, bathing, drying, nails, ears and shedding — and says nothing about
clipping or scissoring at home. That is where the two clippers, the shears, the
hammock and the arm actually belong. They have no editorial home until that
article exists.
