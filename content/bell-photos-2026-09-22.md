# Real photographs on five guides — 22 September 2026

The owner uploaded 33 photographs of Bell to `wp-content/uploads/bell-source/Bell/`.
Five are now the featured image on five guides, replacing supplier product shots.

## Why this was worth doing

Every guide opened with an AliExpress product photograph on a white background.
They were the featured image because something had to be. The site's one genuine
asset that no competitor can copy is a real dog belonging to the person writing,
and none of it was on the page.

## The captions, and why they are not decoration

Each attachment carries a caption, rendered under the photo by
`lookdog-guide-photo.php`. The captions are the point of the exercise.

The clearest case is Safe Play. That guide contains an explicit warning about
tennis balls — the felt is abrasive and wears teeth down over years of fetching —
and the photograph at the top of it is Bell holding a tennis ball. Uncaptioned,
that is a contradiction a reader would notice and hold against the writing. The
caption says so outright: she has one anyway, and the toy a dog picks is rarely
the toy the advice would pick. The photograph goes from a liability to the most
credible thing on the page.

The bath caption opens "Not a bath, but the same principle", because it is a
grooming table and pretending otherwise would be the kind of small lie the house
voice exists to prevent. What it does illustrate is the article's actual
argument: fix the footing first and watch how much of the fighting was ever
about the water.

## Crops

Four originals are 1200×1600 portrait. A portrait featured image renders at
768×1024 and pushes the opening paragraph off the screen, so each was cropped to
4:3 (1200×900) and the crop checked by eye before upload rather than trusting the
offsets. The grooming photograph needed no crop; it was already 1600×1200.

## What the library still cannot do

Worth recording, because it decides what to ask for next.

1. No photograph shows Bell with a product the site sells. Every toy in frame is
   her own. Product pages still depend entirely on supplier images.
2. Every photograph is an "after": groomed, posed, bow in. The guides are about
   problems — matted coat, shedding, pulling on the lead, a dog in a car, a dog
   in a bath — and there is no photograph of any of them.
3. No hands. Nobody brushing, clipping a nail, or fitting a harness. One such
   photograph would be worth more to a grooming guide than ten portraits.
4. One landscape original in 33. A 16:9 header or a desktop hero has exactly one
   candidate, and it is now in use.
