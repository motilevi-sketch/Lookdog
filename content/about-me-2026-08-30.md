## What it is built from

Everything factual in it came from the owner: forty-seven years old, has lived
with dogs essentially all of them, currently a five-year-old Shih Tzu, plus other
people's dogs he looks after. The rest is expansion — the shape of the lessons,
not new biography. **Nothing was invented about his life**: no childhood dog, no
place, no job, no incident. Where the piece needed texture it drew on breed facts
that are true of any Shih Tzu (a big coat that mats against the skin, a short face
that changes the heat rules, a small body that is easy to overfeed).

The one place to check is the list of seven mistakes. He said he made mistakes and
wanted others spared them, but not which. They are written in the first person and
are the near-universal ones — buying gear to fix behaviour, wrong sizing, ignoring
the teeth, believing the label, waiting for it to settle, confusing price with
quality, treating the symptom. Any that are not his should be struck or swapped.

## Deliberate choices

- **It says plainly that he is not a vet or a trainer, in its second section.**
  A personal-authority page that does not disclaim is a liability, and the honesty
  is also the point of the site.
- **The affiliate money is explained in the article, not buried.** It states that
  you can read everything and buy nothing, and suggests buying locally instead.
- **Seven internal links**, all verified 200: pulling, chewing, teeth, heat,
  puppy, shop-by-problem, start.
- **Not in the homepage columns.** The band there is "Read before you buy
  anything"; this is who is doing the writing. It appears as its own line above
  the columns: "Who writes this, and why it is free →". Set by the
  `lookdog_about_post` option, so it is one value to change, not a hardcoded ID.

## Update, 31 August: the real dog

The owner uploaded a photograph of his own Shih Tzu, which replaces the borrowed
one throughout. Attachment 5093 is now the post's featured image and the figure
in the body; the old `lookdog-hero-my-dog.jpg` no longer appears on the page.

Three details worth keeping:

- **It is constrained to 420px and centred.** The source is 988×1600 portrait,
  and at the article's full 68ch measure it would have run to roughly 1,100
  pixels tall — a billboard where a portrait was wanted. The inline `max-width`
  overrides the base rule that stretches block children to the measure.
- **The caption carries content rather than describing the picture**: "My Shih
  Tzu, five years old. That coat is a daily job, not a weekly one." — the same
  point the article makes two paragraphs later.
- **Real alt text**, describing the coat, the tiled floor and the topknots,
  because a photograph of the author is exactly the image a screen reader user
  has no other way to reach.

His name is still not in the piece, because nobody has said it. One line in the
caption is all it would take.

The dashboard action item asking to check this photo is ticked; five remain.

## Update, 31 August: his name is Bell

Named in three places rather than sprinkled through: the opening paragraph
("Right now that dog is Bell, a Shih Tzu, five years old"), the section that
opens the breed argument ("Bell is a small dog carrying a large coat and a short
face"), and the photo caption. Also in the excerpt, which is what shows on the
blog listing, and in the alt text.

The photograph's file was renamed from `WhatsApp-Image-2026-08-26-at-11.47.52.jpeg`
to `bell-shih-tzu.jpg`, with its eight generated sizes regenerated and the eight
old ones deleted. A filename is a ranking signal on image search and the only
part of an image a crawler can read without help; a WhatsApp timestamp says
nothing about a dog. Worth doing now, while nothing links to the old URL.

rename has now removed. If a numeral was seen somewhere else, it has not been
